### Riley

Riley approached WeShould more deliberately, often checking what an action meant before moving forward. When saving the Leopard Lounge reel, she found "I'd Go" but asked, "Does clicking I'd Go tell my friends I want to do this, or is it just saving it for me?" She understood how to save something but wanted to know who would see it.

When looking for activities to do with friends, Riley identified Paint and Sip as an overlap with Theo and the Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum as one with Nora and Theo. She understood that they had independently saved the museum rather than invited her, which suggested the matching feature was clear. However, after accepting a connection request, she asked whether that person could now see everything she'd saved. The boundaries around sharing weren't obvious to her.

During the Gardner Museum planning task, Riley questioned whether "Friday evening" was specific enough. I had intentionally allowed flexible scheduling, but her hesitation showed that some guidance would be useful. She also checked that the proposal was still waiting on Nora and Theo rather than already confirmed. In the next task, she correctly understood that her acceptance was the final one needed to commit the plan.

Riley distinguished cancelling Thursday's outing from losing interest in the activity and expected completed plans to appear separately from upcoming ones. Both matched the app's intended behavior.

Her session showed me that the underlying workflow was understandable, but some actions needed clearer explanations before users took them. I would prioritize visibility information, more prominent pending statuses, and optional scheduling guidance rather than adding extra steps.
