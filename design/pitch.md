# Application Pitch

## WeShould

### From “we should totally do this” to something you actually do.

My friends and I already find plenty of things we want to try.

The problem is we usually find them while we're doing something else.

I see a pottery class on TikTok and think, *I'd go*. I send it to a friend, she responds “omg yes,” and then neither of us have the opportunity to stop what we're doing and choose a Saturday three weeks from now.

One week later, we've both completely forgotten about it.

**WeShould saves the intention at the exact moment it happens.**

## 1. I'd Go

When you see a restaurant, class, concert, or activity on another app, use the normal **Share** button and choose **WeShould**.

A tiny panel appears:

> Wally's Cafe  
> **I'd Go**

Tap once.

Done.

No date. No guest list. No calendar. No event creation.

The point is to record genuine willingness almost as easy as liking the original post.

## 2. You Both Would

WeShould remembers those intentions.

When someone you are connected with independently expresses interest in the same activity, the app surfaces the overlap:

> **You + Marie would both go**  
> Wally's Cafe

Instead of asking every friend whether they want to try boxing, pottery, or a jazz bar, users can discover interest that already exists.

This turns a private “maybe someday” into a social opportunity without requiring either person to publicly recruit the other.

## 3. Make It Real

Only after shared interest exists does WeShould ask users to plan.

Either person can propose a time. The others can accept or decline. When everyone agrees the activity becomes a committed plan.

If tickets, reservations, or registration are required, WeShould directs users to the appropriate external site.

The interaction deliberately follows this order:

```text
discover → I'd Go → we both would → make a plan
```

The application doesn't introduce planning friction until there is evidence that planning is actually worth doing.

WeShould isn't trying to give people more things to scroll through; it's trying to rescue what they already wanted to do from the scroll.
